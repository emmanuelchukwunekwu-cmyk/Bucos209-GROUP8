## 1. The Problem
Students have difficulty finding venues on campus, and maintenance requests can be delayed or receive no response. Many students have to call a senior student or ask people for directions while walking around campus. Students also report that maintenance issues are not addressed quickly or are not answered at all. First-year students are affected most, but students in other levels experience these problems too.
### 1.1 Problem statement
Students struggle to find class venues on campus and to get maintenance problems addressed in a timely way.
### 1.2 Where and how I observed it
I observed these problems through a survey of 17 students across 100, 200, and 300 Level. The survey asked about maintenance experiences and reporting, difficulty finding venues, and students' interest in apps that could help with these issues.
### 1.3 Why this problem matters
When students cannot find a venue, they may need to call a senior student or stop people to ask for directions. When maintenance requests are delayed or ignored, students have to live with unresolved issues. These problems affect first-year students most, while students in other levels also report experiencing them.

## 2. Who Has the Problem
### 2.1 Primary users
The main users of the app are students who are dealing with either of the two problems. Some students may have maintenance issues in their hostel or around school, such as a faulty light, leaking tap, broken fan or damaged facility, but may not know the right person to report it to or may report it and the problem never get fixed . Others, especially freshers, may have trouble finding places on campus because they are not yet familiar with the school environment. They may need to locate lecture halls, offices, hostels, the library or other buildings. The app is meant to help students report these problems and find places around campus more easily.
### 2.2 Secondary users (if any)
The secondary users include hostel supervisors, maintenance workers and other school staff who are responsible for attending to reported faults. They may need to receive complaints, check the details of the problems and keep track of issues that have been reported so that they are not easily forgotten. Having reports in one place could also make it easier for them to know which problems still need attention.  
Returning students can also be secondary users because they are more familiar with the school and may help freshers with information about different locations. Security personnel and other staff who know the campus may also assist students who need directions. They are included because they can help make it easier for new students to find their way around school.
### 2.3 User profile / persona
One example of a user is a student staying in a hostel who notices that a tap is leaking or a light has stopped working and wants to report it without having to keep searching for someone to tell. Another example is a fresher who has a lecture in a building they have not been to before and does not know how to get there. These are the kinds of situations the app is expected to help with, by making maintenance reporting and finding campus locations easier.

## 3. Research
### 3.1 How people cope today
When students notice a maintenance problem, they may report it directly to a hostel supervisor, maintenance staff, write a maintenance report form or another person they think can help. Sometimes, they may have to report the same problem again or rely on someone else to pass the complaint on. This can make it hard to know whether the complaint has been received or if anyone is working on it.  
Freshers who are trying to find places around campus usually ask their roommates, coursemates, landmarks, senior students or security personnel for directions. Some may also use Google Maps or follow other students to a venue. These methods can help, but they do not always give the exact directions needed, especially when the place is unfamiliar.
### 3.2 Existing solutions
- **Reporting to hostel supervisors or filling maintenance form**
  - What it does: Students report faults or facility problems to the people responsible for handling them.
  - Where it falls short: A complaint may be forgotten or students may not know whether it has been attended to.
- **Maintenance complaint form**
  - What it does: Students fill complaint forms for maintenance
  - Where it falls short: Forms may be missed and it can be difficult to keep track of what has been reported.
- **Google Maps**
  - What it does: Helps users find roads, buildings and some general campus locations.
  - Where it falls short: It may not show specific lecture halls, offices or smaller locations clearly.
- **Asking senior students or coursemates**
  - What it does: Freshers get directions from students who already know the school.
  - Where it falls short: The person may not be around when needed, and directions may not always be clear.
- **Campus signboards**
  - What it does: Help students identify buildings and some facilities.
  - Where it falls short: Some places may not be clearly labelled, and signs may not show how to get to a specific room or venue.
- **Asking security personnel**
  - What it does: Security staff can give directions to buildings and other places on campus.
  - Where it falls short: Students may still have to ask each time they need to find somewhere unfamiliar.

  ## 4. Interviews and Surveys
### 4.1 Method
We used a survey to learn about students' experiences with maintenance problems and finding class venues. The survey asked about students' level of study, maintenance issues and reporting, difficulty locating venues, and interest in apps that could help. The data provided does not say how participants were selected.
### 4.2 Participants
The survey received 17 responses. Participants were students in 100, 200, and 300 Level:
- 100 Level: 7 responses
- 200 Level: 9 responses
- 300 Level: 1 response

One response did not include an answer about maintenance experience; some other answers were also left blank.
### 4.3 Questions asked
1. Have you experienced any maintenance problems? What were they, where did they occur, how did you report them, and how long did it take to get a response?
2. How much do unattended maintenance problems affect your life as a student?
3. Have you ever gotten lost trying to find a venue? Which places were hardest to find, and how did you find your first class?
4. Would you be interested in an app to help you find class venues?
5. Would you use an app to report maintenance problems and get them attended to quickly?
### 4.4 What people said
- Of the 16 students who answered the maintenance question, 12 said they had experienced a problem and 4 said they had not.
- Reported problems included hostel room and toilet issues, plumbing, faulty lights and switches, ventilation, tap water, and internet access.
- Students mentioned reporting problems to a hall admin, in person, or by paper form. Some reported delays ranging from a few minutes to a month; others said they had received no response or had not yet received an answer.
- 11 of the 16 students who answered the lost-venue question said they had gotten lost. Lecture classes and offices were commonly named as difficult places to find.
- Students said they found their first class by asking course mates or seniors, following signs, or using Google Maps.
- Of the 15 students who answered the venue-app question, 14 were interested and 1 was not.
- All 16 students who answered the maintenance-app question said they would use it.
### 4.5 Key insights
Students reported a range of maintenance problems, and some described long waits or no response. This suggests that reporting and tracking repairs may be a useful feature for a student app.  
Finding lecture venues and offices was also a recurring challenge. Students often relied on other people or signs to find their way, and nearly all respondents who answered the question were interested in an app to locate class venues.  
Interest in both app ideas was high among respondents. The survey suggests these features could address problems students experience, though the results represent a small group and do not show how participants were selected.

## 5. Proposed Solution
### 5.1 Solution overview
We're building a simple campus app that does two things using the same map.  
First, it helps freshers find their way. Search for a building, lecture hall or office, and the app shows where it is, what landmark to look for, and a photo so you know you're at the right place.  
Second, it lets anyone report a broken thing by picking the exact spot on the map. You choose the location, say what's wrong, add a photo if you like, and then you can watch the request move from Submitted to Assigned to In progress to Resolved.  
We're putting these together because they're really the same problem: people can't connect a place on campus with the right information or the right person. A report pinned to a real location is harder to lose or misunderstand than "the AC in the lecture hall is broken". And freshers will open the map in week one, so people already have the app when something breaks.  
The gap from 3.3: It solves the problem by adding landmarks and photos of the places where lectures will hold, instead of only showing the location using Google Maps as described in 3.3.
### 5.2 How it addresses what users told you
- **Searchable campus map**
  - What users told us: Freshers mostly ask other people or guess
  - Evidence: 14 of 15 respondents wanted a venue-finding app.
- **Landmarks and photos for tricky places**
  - What users told us: Some locations were much harder to find than others
  - Evidence: 11 of 16 had got lost finding a venue; lecture halls and offices were hardest.
- **Report-by-location form**
  - What users told us: Requests end up vague or sent to the wrong person
  - Evidence: 12 of 16 had a maintenance problem; all 16 would use a reporting app.
- **Status tracking**
  - What users told us: A lot of requests never got a reply
  - Evidence: Replies took minutes to a month, and some got none.
- **Reminders to maintenance staff**
  - What users told us: People chase repairs by calling, asking friends or going back in person
  - Evidence: Some reports waited up to a month or got no reply.

## 6. MVP 
### 6.1 What is in the MVP
For the first version, we're keeping it small:
- A campus map with search, starting with the 10 to 15 hardest-to-find places from our survey rather than the whole campus
- A short "how to find it" note, landmark and photo for each of those places
- A repair request form with location, type of problem, a short description and an optional photo
- A status page where the person who reported can see where their request stands
- A basic admin page where maintenance staff can see requests and update them
- Sign-in with a student email, so reports can be traced and spam stays down
### 6.2 What is NOT in the MVP (and why)
- Live turn-by-turn GPS: It's expensive to build, and a map with good landmarks might be enough.
- Indoor floor-by-floor maps: It takes too long to survey every building. We'll add it if people say they get lost inside.
- Automatic routing to technicians: We'd need staff data and buy-in. Assigning by hand is enough to test the idea.
- Push notifications: People can check status in the app for now.
- Upvoting or merging duplicate reports: Useful once lots of people use it, but not needed to prove the idea.
- Full campus coverage: We start with the most-searched places and grow from there.
- Management analytics: Nice later, but it doesn't solve the students' problem.
### 6.3 User stories
- As a fresher, I want to search for a building or hall and see how to find it, so that I don't turn up late to class.
- As a fresher, I want landmarks and photos for each place, so that I can tell I've got the right one.
- As a student, I want to report a fault by picking its location and adding a photo, so that maintenance knows exactly what's wrong and where.
- As a student, I want to see the status of my request, so that I don't have to chase people to find out if anyone has seen it.
- As a maintenance staff member, I want to see all open requests with their location and photo, so that I can prioritise and update them in one place.
- As a hall or facility manager, I want to see which requests are still unresolved, so that I can spot problems getting missed

## 7. Requirements
### 7.1 Functional (the system shall)
- **FR1:** Register and authenticate students securely
- **FR2:** Authenticate administrators securely
- **FR3:** Accept maintenance complaints from students, with a category and description
- **FR4:** Display the status of each complaint to the student who filed it
- **FR5:** Display hostel and room details to students
- **FR6:** Let admins view, manage and update the status of complaints
- **FR7:** Notify students by email when their complaint status changes
### 7.2 Non-functional
- **Performance:** pages load within 3 seconds under normal use
- **Security:** passwords are stored encrypted, and students can only see their own data
- **Reliability and availability:** at least 99% uptime
- **Usability:** a student can submit a complaint in 5 steps or fewer
- **Scalability:** supports a growing number of students without a drop in performance
### 7.3 Data to store
1. Student details (name, matric number, email, room assignment)
2. Login credentials (encrypted passwords)
3. Hostel info (hostel names, room numbers)
4. Complaints (description, category, priority)
5. Submission date and date resolved
6. Complaint status (Pending, In Progress, Resolved)
7. Administrator details, and which admin handled each complaint
8. Notifications
## 8. Approach (before choosing a language)
Start by identifying the problems students face with hostel maintenance, such as complaints getting lost or going unanswered. Then gather requirements from students and wardens and sketch the database layout.
Split the system into these parts:
1. **User Registration and Login:** creates accounts and authenticates students and admins
2. **Student Dashboard:** shows hostel and room info and complaint status
3. **Complaint Management:** lets students submit complaints
4. **Administrator Dashboard:** lets admins view, manage and update complaints 
5. **Database:** stores student, hostel, complaint and admin data
6. **Notification System:** alerts students when a complaint is updated and admin also recieves a notification if a complaint is made

**How they interact:** Student registers and logs in → submits a complaint → it is saved in the database → admin sees it on their dashboard and updates the status → the database records the change → the notification system emails the student → the student sees the new status on their dashboard.
**Goal:** replace word-of-mouth and paper complaints with a trackable system that makes hostel maintenance faster and better organised.

## 9. Assumptions and Risks
### Assumptions
1. **Assumption:** We can get accurate map pins for key halls and hostels early on.
   - **Why it matters:** Bad pins break trust and send complaints to the wrong spot.
   - **How to handle it:** Start with core buildings/hostels using clean location IDs so we can add more later.
2. **Assumption:** Porters and hall admins will actually update ticket statuses (Submitted → In Progress → Resolved) instead of only using logbooks.
   - **Why it matters:** Right now students meet porters to write in logbooks, so tickets will stall if nobody updates the app.
   - **How to handle it:** Keep the admin view dead simple with hostel filters, one-click status changes, and familiar logbook fields.
### Risks
3. **Risk:** Not enough students show up to test the MVP.
   - **Why it matters:** We'll miss clunky flows before grading or demo day.
   - **How to handle it:** Grab roommates, coursemates, and hostel peers for quick 5-min tests using real tasks.
4. **Risk:** Doing both navigation and maintenance on a tight schedule leaves half-baked features.
   - **Why it matters:** Scope creep can sink the deadline or cause messy merge bugs.
   - **How to handle it:** Lock v1 down to core map search + ticket tracking, split tasks clearly in Group 12, and use branches.
5. **Risk:** Duplicate or vague complaints pile up in the queue.
   - **Why it matters:** Multiple roommates reporting the same thing slows porters down.
   - **How to handle it:** Force hostel/room selection and show open issues for that spot first.
## 10. Success Metrics
Testing via a 15 to 20 student peer session + live demo:

1. **Pin speed:** At least 85% of testers find an unfamiliar spot in under 60s without asking around.
2. **Status flow:** 100% of test tickets save with location/room info and update cleanly (Submitted → In Progress → Resolved).
3. **Admin speed:** Porters/admins filter by hostel and change status in under 20s (3 clicks max).
4. **Core demo:** Search, pins, ticket creation, and status updates all run with zero big bugs during testing and demo.
## 11. Future Improvements
- **Full campus coverage:** grow from the most-searched places to every hall, hostel and key building on campus.
- **Upvoting and merging duplicate reports:** let students upvote an existing complaint, and merge duplicates once many people are using the system.
- **Push notifications:** alert students and porters instantly instead of relying on checking status in the app.
- **Photo uploads:** let students attach a picture of the fault (broken bulb, leaking tap) so porters know what to fix before they arrive.
- **Priority and urgency levels:** let urgent faults (electrical, water) jump to the top of the queue.
## 12. Conclusion
The Hostel Management and Maintenance System replaces logbooks and word-of-mouth complaints with a trackable digital system. Students can find hostels and halls on the map, report maintenance problems with the exact hostel and room, and follow each complaint from Submitted to In Progress to Resolved. Porters and hall admins get a simple dashboard where they can filter by hostel and update statuses in a few clicks. By keeping version 1 focused on core map search and ticket tracking, and testing it with real students, the project aims to deliver a working MVP that makes hostel navigation and maintenance faster, clearer and better organised. The future improvements can then be added step by step as more students and staff start using it.
